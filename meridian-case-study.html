A UX case study for a public museum experience, designed around accessibility, clearer planning, and confident independent booking.

UX Research App Accessibility

Project overview

The product:

A museum companion app and responsive website that help visitors explore exhibitions, find access information, and book visits independently.

View prototype
UNDERSTANDING THE USER

Research: who gets left out, and why

Six qualitative interviews, grounded in equity-focused reasoning rather than a guess.

The brief didn't name a specific audience, so I ran a business-requirements conversation with the museum, then applied equity-focused reasoning to find who was underserved. Older adults and visitors with visual, hearing, or mobility-related disabilities emerged as the primary research focus. I recruited six participants with a screener survey and conducted 15–20 minute interviews to understand their real planning and booking experiences.

Hard-to-find accessibility info

Seating, ramps, and parking details are missing or buried on museum websites.

Aa

Small text, low contrast

Text sizing and color contrast make pages difficult to read for low-vision visitors.

CC

No video captions

Promotional and virtual-tour videos rarely include captions or transcripts.

Confusing booking flow

Uncertainty about whether a booking went through, with no clear confirmation.

Persona: James

Problem statement:

James is a retired teacher with low vision who needs to plan and book museum visits independently because accessibility information is often hard to find before arriving.

Journey map

Goal:

James wants to find accessibility and practical museum information, then book a visit independently with clear confirmation at every step.

ACCESSIBILITY

Designed to WCAG AA, not bolted on after

Considered at every stage, not patched in at the end.

Color & contrast

Every primary color pairing checked against WCAG AA before any UI was built.

CC

Captions by default

All video plays with captions on, backed by a full searchable transcript.

Aa

Clear text hierarchy

Two typefaces only, with a consistent size and weight system throughout.

Simple, predictable nav

Fewer choices per screen, with accessibility info placed where it's remembered.
